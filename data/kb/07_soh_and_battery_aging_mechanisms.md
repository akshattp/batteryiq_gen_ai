# State of Health (SOH) and Battery Aging Mechanisms

## Definition of State of Health
State of Health (SOH) quantifies the present degradation state of a battery compared to its fresh, unaged condition:
SOH_capacity (%) = (Q_present / Q_nominal) * 100
When SOH drops below 80% (or internal resistance increases by 100%), the battery is conventionally considered to have reached its End-of-Life (EOL) for electric vehicle traction duty.

## Primary Degradation Mechanisms
1. **SEI Layer Growth:** Continuous side reactions between electrolyte solvent and active anode material consume recyclable cyclable lithium ions, permanently reducing capacity.
2. **Electrode Mechanical Cracking:** Repeated expansion and contraction of graphite and NMC crystalline lattices during intercalation/de-intercalation induces micro-cracking and loss of active material.
3. **Transition Metal Dissolution:** Manganese and nickel ions dissolve into the electrolyte and migrate to the anode, catalyzing further SEI growth.
4. **Current Collector Corrosion:** Oxidation of copper or aluminum foil leads to increased contact resistance and sudden voltage drops under load.
