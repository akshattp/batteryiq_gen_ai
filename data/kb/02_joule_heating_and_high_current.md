# Joule Heating and High Current Thermal Dynamics

## The Physics of Resistive Heat Generation
Electrical current flowing through a lithium-ion cell dissipates heat according to Joule's First Law:
P_joule = I^2 * R_internal
where I is the electric current (in Amperes) and R_internal is the cell internal equivalent series resistance (ESR in Ohms). Because heat generation scales quadratically with current, doubling the operating current quadruples internal heat production.

## Total Thermal Generation Components
The total heat generation in a lithium-ion cell comprises two primary components:
1. **Irreversible Joule (Ohmic) Heating:** Generated by current overcoming charge-transfer resistance, electrolyte bulk resistance, and electronic resistance of electrodes and current collectors.
2. **Reversible Entropic (Reaction) Heating:** Arises from the entropy change of electrochemical intercalation reactions.

## Busbar and Interconnect Thermal Hotspots
In high-current battery packs, loose contact bolts or poor weld quality between cell terminals and copper busbars create localized contact resistance. Under sustained load of 30 A to 50 A, contact resistances as small as 5 mOhm can generate significant localized hotspots that conduct directly into cell headers, precipitating localized thermal degradation.
