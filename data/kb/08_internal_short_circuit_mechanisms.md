# Internal Short Circuit (ISC) Mechanisms and Signatures

## Etiology of Internal Shorts
An Internal Short Circuit (ISC) occurs when the separator between cathode and anode fails, allowing electronic conduction inside the cell:
- **Lithium Dendrites:** Tree-like metallic lithium needles growing during overcharging or cold-temperature charging pierce through separator pores.
- **Microscopic Metallic Contaminants:** Burrs on slit current collector edges or dust particles introduced during cell manufacturing penetrate the separator under mechanical pressure.
- **Mechanical Deformation:** External crushing or impact deforms cell jellyrolls, driving foil contacts.

## Diagnostic Precursor Signatures
- **Accelerated Self-Discharge:** Cell voltage sags faster than neighboring cells during resting or low-load phases.
- **Anomalous Thermal Disconnect:** Cell temperature rises without commensurate operational current (exothermic self-discharge dissipation).
- **Reduced Coulombic Efficiency:** Apparent charge required to reach full voltage is abnormally high due to internal leakage current.
