# Thermal Runaway Fundamentals in Lithium-ion Cells

## Overview and Initiation
Thermal runaway in lithium-ion batteries is a self-accelerating exothermic chain reaction where cell internal heat generation significantly exceeds the rate of heat dissipation to the surroundings. Once initiated, internal temperatures escalate rapidly, leading to separator collapse, explosive gas release, and intense fire.

## Chemical Reaction Sequence
1. **SEI Layer Breakdown (80 °C – 120 °C):** The protective Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) layer on the graphite anode decomposes exothermically. The exposed lithiated graphite begins reacting directly with organic solvent molecules.
2. **Anode and Solvent Reaction (120 °C – 140 °C):** Rapid reaction between the intercalated lithium and organic carbonates generates flammable hydrocarbons (ethylene, methane) and increases cell internal pressure.
3. **Separator Melting (130 °C – 170 °C):** Standard polyethylene (PE) or polypropylene (PP) microporous separators melt, creating direct physical contact between positive and negative electrodes, triggering massive internal short circuits.
4. **Cathode Decomposition and Oxygen Egress (180 °C – 250 °C):** Transition metal oxide cathodes (NMC, LCO) break down, liberating combustible oxygen directly into the electrolyte vapor.
5. **Electrolyte Combustion (>200 °C):** Co-presence of oxygen, organic solvent vapors, and extreme heat drives vigorous self-sustaining combustion and potential cell rupture.

## Prevention and Diagnostic Thresholds
Thermal runaway can be preempted by detecting thermal acceleration (dT/dt > 2 °C/min) and enforcing protective cutoffs before the initial SEI breakdown threshold (around 50 °C to 60 °C).
